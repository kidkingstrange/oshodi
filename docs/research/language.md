# Research Note: Language & Idioms in Oshodi
Date researched: 2026-10-31
Summary (own words):
Everyday street discourse in Oshodi mixes Nigerian Pidgin, Lagos street slang, Yoruba loanwords, and English. Tone ranges from rapid banter, affectionate greetings ("Oga!", "Madam!"), bargaining rhetoric ("How much last?"), to cautionary advice ("Look your back").

Key phrases and vocabulary:
- "No wahala" / "Wahala no dey finish": No problem / Daily struggles are endless.
- "How body?": Friendly greeting asking after someone's welfare.
- "Drop your balance": Conductor asking for passenger change or fare.
- "Sharp sharp": Quickly, without delay.
- "Hold your side": Be vigilant in crowded spaces.
- "Light don come": Electricity has been restored from the national grid.
- "Up Nepa / PHCN": Spontaneous street cheer when power returns.
- "Oya enter with your change": Bus conductor instruction to board with exact fare.

Tone guidance:
- Warm, observant, witty, and resilient.
- Avoid mockery or stereotyping; showcase neighbourhood solidarity, work ethic, and quick wit.

Ideas for Dialogue & Quests:
- Baba Landlord reminding tenants about meter maintenance dues.
- Mama Nkechi checking in on tired hawkers and drivers.
- Chairman Goke arbitrating bus loading disputes with calm authority.
